Key Terms

Asset Classification

The process of categorizing information and assets based on their sensitivity and criticality to the organization.

Data Owner

Business unit manager or executive responsible for defining classification levels, determining access rights, and accepting residual risk.

Data Custodian

IT staff responsible for implementing security controls, maintaining systems, and executing backup procedures.

Data Steward

Subject matter expert who ensures data quality, integrity, and compliance with policies.

Chain of Custody

Documented trail showing who had possession of information or evidence at all times.

Encryption

Transformation of data into an unreadable format without the proper decryption key.

Tokenization

Replacement of sensitive data with non-sensitive placeholders to reduce compliance scope.

Data Loss Prevention (DLP)

Technology that monitors and controls data movement in three states: in use, in motion, and at rest.

Media Sanitization

Process of removing data from storage media to prevent recovery, including clearing, purging, and destroying.

Data Remanence

Residual data that remains after attempted removal, posing a potential security risk.

Privacy by Design

Building privacy protections into systems from the start rather than adding them later.

Legal Hold

Suspension of normal retention and destruction policies when litigation is reasonably anticipated.

Quantitative Valuation

Asset valuation method assigning monetary values based on cost, replacement value, or revenue generation.

Qualitative Valuation

Asset valuation based on criticality, sensitivity, regulatory requirements, or competitive advantage.

CISSP Domain 2: Asset Security Study Guide

This domain represents 10% of the CISSP exam and focuses on the protection of organizational information and assets.

Scenario: Imagine you are the newly appointed CISO of a multinational financial services company. Where do you begin?

1.1 Asset Identification and Valuation

💡 Analogy: The Library Catalog System

Before a library can protect its books, it must catalog what it has and their value. Similarly, organizations must inventory all assets before implementing protection measures.

Quantitative Valuation
  • Original cost
  • Replacement cost
  • Revenue generation
  • Cost of loss
Qualitative Valuation
  • Business criticality
  • Information sensitivity
  • Regulatory requirements
  • Competitive advantage

1.2 Data and Asset Classification

💡 Analogy: Airport Security Lanes

TSA PreCheck travelers go through expedited screening while others face standard procedures. Data classification similarly determines security control levels.

Restricted/Confidential

Highest sensitivity; severe damage if disclosed

Private/Internal

Internal use only; moderate damage if disclosed

Sensitive

Requires protection; limited damage

Public

Freely disclosed with no impact

1.3 Asset and Data Ownership

💡 Analogy: The Restaurant Kitchen

Owner decides menu (Data Owner), head chef ensures quality (Data Steward), line cooks prepare food (Data Custodians), servers deliver (Users).

Data Owners

Business unit managers or executives

  • Define classification levels
  • Determine access rights
  • Accept residual risk
Data Custodians

IT staff responsible for implementation

  • Implement security controls
  • Maintain systems
  • Execute backups
Data Stewards

Subject matter experts

  • Ensure data quality
  • Define standards
  • Monitor compliance
Users

All employees and contractors

  • Follow policies
  • Handle information correctly
  • Report incidents

2.2 Data Protection Methods

Encryption

Transforms data into unreadable format without the proper key

Tokenization

Example: Card 4532-1234-5678-9010 becomes ABC123XYZ789

Data Loss Prevention (DLP)

Data in Use
Controls user actions
Data in Motion
Monitors transmissions
Data at Rest
Protects stored data

Privacy Impact

Assets have a lifecycle from creation to disposal. Security must be maintained throughout this entire journey.

💡 Analogy: The Product Journey

Like a product moving from manufacturing to warehouse to store to customer to recycling, data moves through phases requiring different security controls at each stage.

4.1 Data Lifecycle Phases

Creation/Acquisition

Define security requirements, classify data, implement access controls

Usage

Authentication, authorization, monitoring, logging, encryption

Storage

Secure locations, encryption, backup procedures

Archiving

Long-term retention, compressed storage, periodic verification

Disposal

Secure deletion, hardware sanitization, destruction verification

4.2 Asset Retention

Organizations must develop retention policies that:

  • ✓ Meet regulatory compliance requirements
  • ✓ Support business operational needs
  • ✓ Balance storage costs with retention needs
Legal Holds

Suspension of normal retention policies when litigation is anticipated. Records must be preserved until the hold is lifted.

4.3 End-of-Life and Media Sanitization

💡 Analogy: Shredding Documents

Just as sensitive paper documents require shredding, digital media requires proper sanitization methods based on sensitivity and media type.

Media Sanitization Techniques:

Clearing

Overwriting data with patterns. Suitable for reuse within organization.

Purging

More thorough than clearing. Uses degaussing or cryptographic erasure.

Destroying

Physical destruction: shredding, incinerating, pulverizing. No data recovery possible.

Data Remanence

Residual data remaining after attempted removal. Critical risk requiring proper sanitization techniques to address.

5.1 Data Security Controls

Control selection depends on the state of the data:

Data at Rest
  • Encryption
  • Access controls
  • Physical security
  • Backup protection
Data in Transit
  • TLS/SSL encryption
  • VPN tunnels
  • Secure protocols
  • Network segmentation
Data in Use
  • Application controls
  • Memory protection
  • Rights management
  • DLP monitoring
Control Selection Considerations:
• Security requirements and risk level
• Operational impact and usability
• Cost-effectiveness and ROI
• Compliance requirements

5.2 Compliance Requirements

Different industries face different compliance requirements:

Healthcare (HIPAA)

Protected Health Information (PHI) security and privacy requirements

Financial (PCI DSS, SOX)

Payment card data protection and financial reporting controls

Privacy (GDPR, CCPA)

Personal data protection and individual privacy rights

Government (FISMA, FedRAMP)

Federal information security standards and cloud service requirements

Compliance Process:
  • 1. Regular audits and assessments
  • 2. Documentation of control implementation
  • 3. Evidence of ongoing compliance
  • 4. Remediation of identified gaps
