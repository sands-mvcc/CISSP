CISSP Domain 1 | Security & Risk Management

Key Terms Glossary

These are the fundamental terms you must master for CISSP Domain 1.

CIA Triad
The fundamental model for security, standing for **Confidentiality**, **Integrity**, and **Availability**.
Due Diligence / Due Care
**Due Care** is acting prudently and following what others would do. **Due Diligence** is investigating and researching to maintain the care standard (e.g., conducting regular audits).
Authenticity / Non-repudiation
**Authenticity** verifies identity/origin. **Non-repudiation** ensures a party cannot deny an action (often achieved via digital signatures and timestamps).
RTO / RPO
**RTO (Recovery Time Objective)** is the maximum tolerable time to restore a business function after a disaster. **RPO (Recovery Point Objective)** is the maximum tolerable amount of data loss, measured in time (e.g., 4 hours of data).
Data Owner / Data Custodian
The **Data Owner** (usually management) is responsible for classification and access. The **Data Custodian** (usually IT staff) implements the controls and maintains the data.
NIST CSF (Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover)
The five core functions of the NIST Cybersecurity Framework, defining the lifecycle of managing cybersecurity risk.

Critical Concepts Review

Focus on the *WHY* and *HOW* these high-level principles function within an organization.

  • The **5 Pillars of Information Security** (CIA + Authenticity/Non-repudiation) and their implementation via technical and administrative controls.
  • The relationship between **Security Governance** and **Business Strategy**, including key roles (**CISO, Data Owner**) and responsibilities.
  • Using **Security Control Frameworks** (**ISO 27001, NIST CSF**) as structured methodologies to manage security.
  • The **Risk Management Lifecycle** (Identification, Assessment, Treatment, Monitoring) and the difference between **Qualitative** and **Quantitative** analysis.
  • The role of **Personnel Security** in the employee lifecycle (screening, separation of duties, mandatory vacations).
  • **Business Continuity Planning (BCP)** and **Disaster Recovery (DR)**, focusing on **BIA, RTO,** and **RPO** determination.
  • Understanding global **Legal and Regulatory Issues** (**GDPR, HIPAA, SOX**) and concepts like **Data Sovereignty** and **Jurisdictional Differences**.

Comprehensive Workshop Textbook: Security and Risk Management

Navigate the core managerial and strategic concepts of security, using the CISO's perspective.

Governance ensures that security efforts align with the organization's mission and risk tolerance. It's the **framework** for decision-making, while management handles execution.

Governance vs. Management

**Analogy:** Governance is the **Board of Directors** setting the company's risk tolerance. Management is the **Operations Team** implementing specific firewalls and patch cycles to meet that tolerance.

Policy Hierarchy

**Policy** (High-level, management mandate) $\rightarrow$ **Standard** (Mandatory rules, e.g., AES-256 must be used) $\rightarrow$ **Procedure** (Step-by-step instructions) $\rightarrow$ **Guideline** (Recommendations).

The (ISC)² Code of Ethics (4 Canons)

Security professionals must adhere to these four canons, which guide ethical behavior and decision-making:

  • Protect society, the commonwealth, and the infrastructure.
  • Act honorably, honestly, justly, responsibly, and legally.
  • Provide diligent and competent service to principals.
  • Advance and protect the profession.

Risk is the probability of a threat exploiting a vulnerability and the resulting loss. We manage it using a continuous lifecycle and two main types of analysis.

Quantitative Risk Analysis (Monetary Focus)

This is the most detailed form, focused on calculating the expected financial loss using three key metrics. This is crucial for justifying security budget expenses.

Tap to View Definition & Formula:
**SLE:** The monetary loss from a single occurrence of a threat event.
**Formula:** $$\text{SLE} = \text{Asset Value} \times \text{Exposure Factor (EF)}$$
**ARO:** The estimated frequency of the threat occurring in one year. (e.g., 0.5 = once every two years, 10 = ten times a year).
**ALE:** The expected monetary loss per year. This is the ultimate metric for cost/benefit analysis.
**Formula:** $$\text{ALE} = \text{SLE} \times \text{ARO}$$

💡 Implementation Example: Calculating Risk

If a critical server is valued at **\$100,000**, and a successful DDoS attack (the threat) is expected to render 60% of its value unusable (**EF = 0.6**), and is expected to occur once every 4 years (**ARO = 0.25**):

  • **SLE:** $\$100,000 \times 0.6 = \text{\$60,000}$
  • **ALE:** $\$60,000 \times 0.25 = \text{\$15,000}$

The company can justify spending up to \$15,000 per year on controls (like DDoS mitigation services) to reduce this specific risk.

**BCP** focuses on sustaining business operations, while **DRP** focuses on restoring the technical infrastructure after an outage. Both start with the **BIA** (Business Impact Analysis).

Alternate Site Strategies

Choosing the right alternate site depends directly on the RTO requirement:

Hot Site: Fully equipped, near-instant recovery. Highest cost. (RTO in hours).
Warm Site: Hardware present, but data/apps must be loaded. Moderate cost/time. (RTO in days).
Cold Site: Space and utilities only. Requires hardware and data to be shipped and installed. Lowest cost. (RTO in weeks).

Legal and Regulatory Context

Understanding global laws is critical, especially when dealing with data across borders:

  • **Data Sovereignty:** The legal requirement that data collected in a country must be stored and processed within that country's borders. (e.g., European data must stay in the EU).
  • **Privacy Laws:** **GDPR** is the gold standard, mandating strict data protection and breach notification rules.

The human element is often the weakest link. Personnel security measures protect the organization across the entire employee lifecycle.

Core Personnel Controls

  • **Separation of Duties:** No single person should be able to complete a high-risk task alone (e.g., the person who writes code cannot approve and deploy it). This protects **Integrity**.
  • **Job Rotation:** Moves employees between roles to reduce fraud and prevent skill stagnation. This allows for detection of irregularities.
  • **Mandatory Vacation:** Forces employees to take leave, ensuring continuity and revealing fraudulent activities that require their continuous presence to hide.
  • **Least Privilege:** Users are granted only the minimum access necessary to perform their job. Directly enforces **Confidentiality**.

🛠️ Practical Implementation: Segregation

In a banking environment, one administrator (Admin A) is responsible for creating new user accounts, but a second administrator (Admin B) is required to *enable* the financial transaction privilege. This enforces **Separation of Duties** and prevents a single bad actor from creating and exploiting a malicious account.
