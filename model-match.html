Model Match: Security Models for the Business

Think like a manager — match each business goal at a small healthcare provider to the security model that actually enforces it.

13% of the CISSP exam 4 rounds easy → hard 10–15 min estimated time Apply Bloom's level Intermediate practitioner

Your mission

Harborview Family Health needs an architecture — not a shopping list

Harborview Family Health is a 90-employee community healthcare provider. After a regional competitor suffered a major data-exfiltration breach, the board — risk-averse and heavily regulated — has asked you, the security manager, to justify the models behind the security architecture, not just the products.

Learning objective

By the end of this activity you will be able to match a stated business goal to the security model that best enforces it — Bell-LaPadula, Biba, Clark-Wilson, Brewer-Nash, non-interference, lattice-based, Graham-Denning, Harrison-Ruzzo-Ullman, and Take-Grant — and explain why in one sentence.

Why this matters: Domain 3 questions rarely ask you to recite a model — they hand you a business goal and expect you to pick the model that enforces it, exactly what a CISO does when defending an architecture to regulators.

How to play

  1. Pick a goal, then pick a model. Click (or Tab + Enter to) a business goal on the left, then the security model on the right that best enforces it.
  2. Struggle first, hint second. A 💡 hint is hidden until you ask for it — but asking costs a little Operational Health (analyst time isn't free).
  3. Two attempts per match. Miss once and you get a hint and one retry; miss twice and the answer is revealed with the full rationale.
  4. Watch the trade-off meters. Correct matches raise Security Effectiveness; wrong attempts and hints drain Operational Health. Finish with both strong.
